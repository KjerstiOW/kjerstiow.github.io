I graduated from Maryville University with a degree in Mathematical Sciences in May 2026. I make an attempt to read, understand, and do some mathematics every day.
After all, Halmos once said: "The only way to learn mathematics is to do mathematics."

My interests are focused around algebraic and arithmetic geometry, category theory, and mathematical logic.
More specifically I enjoy local-global principles (such as Hasse-Minkowski), the boundaries of hypotheses, and connections between different areas of mathematics.
Despite these being my main foci, my interests are rather broad and it would be quicker to name things I am not interested in. 

My philosophy is each object is *itself* a local object in a "global" state of mathematics; weakening and strengthening hypotheses simply move around some local neighborhood.
This also explains my areas of interest.

I typically ask myself after reading a proof: Why does this theorem hold in the first place, and what is the "big idea?" Why are these hypotheses necessary? Can we generalize this somehow, perhaps to other objects or categories? If we weaken the hypotheses, does this give rise to some weaker version of the original theorem?
